How the ranking works

Implied value is calibrated on the — protocols that already trade with a usable market cap. For each category the model takes the median market-cap / TVL ratio of that category's tokened protocols, then applies three fitted adjustments: implied = TVL × peer multiple × size factor × growth factor × maturity factor. The size and growth terms are not hand-set — they are fitted on the peers themselves (least squares on their log ratios, each shrunk halfway to zero). Peer ratios fall as TVL grows (fitted slope —), so the size factor tempers the largest protocols instead of letting a raw multiple overvalue them. A hot week of inflows prices at a discount among peers that already trade (fitted momentum beta —), so the growth factor tempers momentum rather than chasing it; the maturity factor is kept deliberately small, because the peers show almost no age effect. Categories with too few tokened peers fall back to the global median (—). Each protocol's detail view also shows a value range: its category's interquartile peer multiples (25th–75th percentile) put through the same factors.

The model grades itself on this page: re-pricing every peer with that peer left out of the fit, its median miss is a factor of —, against — for a plain median-multiple model with hand-set adjustments — the fitted terms earn their place, and the remaining spread is why every row carries a range, not just a point. Before any ranking, a structural screen sets aside — entries that record no token but are wrapped, staked or bridged products of one that already trades — or an exchange's reserve tracker — using the same signals the snapshot's own watchlist uses: their value already trades under another name, so they have no launch of their own. They are listed, with their reasons, below the board. No name is hand-picked or hand-weighted: feed the model a new snapshot and the board re-derives itself.
